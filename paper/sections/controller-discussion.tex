\paragraph{What reuse establishes.}
The controlled A2 comparison keeps allocated workspaces in both variants.
It isolates the cost of rerunning matching and the previous RANSAC budget.
It does not estimate allocator overhead or a reduction in peak memory.
Retaining search state requires consistent inputs, scale, generation, seed,
solver settings, and completed device work. When these conditions fail,
the affected stages must restart. Pairs accepted on the first attempt do
not benefit from avoiding a retry that never occurred.

\paragraph{Limits of online checks.}
Inlier count, ratio, coverage, and residual constrain the fitted consensus;
they do not test every pixel in the overlap. A repeated texture can support
an incorrect correspondence pattern, and a global homography cannot fit
arbitrary parallax or independently moving objects. A
\texttt{BUDGET\_EXHAUSTED} trace records a terminal decision, not a visual
cause. The accompanying image overlays are diagnostic evidence; they do
not provide independent parallax annotations.

\paragraph{Scope of the measured comparison.}
The reported controller uses a frozen heuristic with feather blending on
one Tesla T4 runtime. The main latency distribution includes rejected
pairs, which can terminate before rendering. Different completion rates
must therefore be considered alongside latency. Without the corresponding
offline overlap-quality assessment, these results do not establish a
quality-constrained end-to-end speedup. No final-test result was used to
adjust the policy. Sensitivity to alternative quality policies and transfer
to other GPU architectures remain separate experiments.
